# P7 — The 8 locks: knowing when it doesn't know

## TLDR

P6 put Craton above ETAS. P7 asks the harder question: when should you *not* believe it?

Eight locks, each a test that a forecaster can fail out loud: is "1%" really 1%? Does it know Sicily is
not central Italy? Does a glitch in the catalogue make it ring alarms? Does it ever break physics? Does
it make you watch less of the map than ETAS does? And the last one, the only one nobody can argue
with: **from tonight, one forecast a day is sealed with a hash and published before the day starts**,
then graded in public.

The referee came out of it too, and its first job was to kill the project's own false discovery from
the day before: aftershocks "locked" to the tide.

Where it stands: to catch 90% of the M4+ quakes of 2009 and 2016-17, Craton makes you watch **0.84%** of
central Italy's space-time, ETAS **1.32%**. It breaks no physics; every ETAS variant does. It knows Sicily is
new (AUROC 0.997), but only thanks to a hand-made fingerprint. It is the worst-calibrated cell by cell of
the ETAS family (0.32 doublings off, against 0.10 for the best floor), and one glitch leaks past its guard
on one seed in three. All of that is on the scoreboard.

## The idea, step by step

**1. Calibration: does 1% happen 1% of the time?** Take every (day, 0.1° cell) of the two test
sequences and the forecast's chance of at least one M3+ quake there. Group them by that chance (0.1-0.3%,
0.3-1%, 1-3%, …) and in each group count how often a quake really came. A perfect forecast's groups sit
on the diagonal. The usual number for the gap is the expected calibration error (ECE), and for rare
events it's a trap: a group forecast at 0.16% that came 0.54% of the time has a gap of 0.004, which
sounds perfect, and is three times too low. So CB-05 measures the gap as a *factor*, in doublings
(log₂ of observed over forecast): 0 is perfect, 1 means "off by 2x".

**2. Clusters break Poisson.** CB-02 scores a forecast as a Poisson rate: a day expected to bring 10
quakes brings about 10, give or take 3. Real days don't: they bring 0, or 60. Put each real day's count
where it falls inside the forecast's distribution (the PIT: probability integral transform). An honest
forecast spreads those positions evenly between 0 and 1; a Poisson forecast of a clustered process piles
them at both ends. The fix is one number per checkpoint: the negative binomial's size k (variance =
mean + mean²/k), fitted on the training split's validation days, never on the test.

**3. Six Fields that argue.** The Field is now six networks from six seeds, averaged: P6's four, kept as
they were, and two new ones. An ensemble grows; it is never retrained (retraining the same four seeds on a
GPU already moves a score by a few hundredths of a bit, and throws away what shipped). Where they agree,
the data was clear; where they disagree, it wasn't (deep ensembles: disagreement is epistemic
uncertainty, the "I don't know" kind, as opposed to the dice-roll kind the negative binomial carries).

**4. "I have never seen this."** Before a forecast, ask whether its context (the 1,024 quakes before
midnight) looks like anything the model learned from: the distance from its vector to the 10th nearest
training context, nearest-neighbour style (Sun et al. 2022). There is a trap here too: the trunk reads
absolute latitude and longitude, so a context from Sicily is "far" just for being south, and any
detector scores a perfect, useless 1.0. Every context is first moved so its median epicentre sits in
central Italy. What remains to tell apart is the seismicity itself.

**5. Physics budgets.** Four things no forecast may do: negative rates; more big quakes than small ones;
forecast *more* quakes on a day with no new quakes (the Omori probe: give it the catalogue up to the end
of Amatrice's first day and then nothing, and watch 30 days); and promise more seismic moment than the
crust can store. That last one is the slip budget: the Apennines stretch by at most ~4 mm a year, and
Kostrov's formula turns that into at most ~1.6 × 10¹⁸ N·m of moment a year.

**6. Fake precursors.** Radon, animals, b-value drops, GPS wiggles: every precursor in history died on an
artifact. CB-07 plants five fakes in the two months before L'Aquila 2009 and counts false alarms. Three
fakes carry no information about the Earth at all (a 5 cm GNSS step, every quake listed twice, every
magnitude 0.4 too high), and the forecast must ignore them. Two are real-looking seismicity (a b-value
drop, a swarm), where reacting can be physics: those are reported, not counted.

**7. The guard.** Two of those fakes are common in real catalogues, so Craton now has an input guard on
the live edge: drop a quake listed twice (within 5 s, 3 km, 0.3 magnitude units), and take off a
magnitude shift that moves the whole distribution everywhere at once while the number of quakes stays
the same (a real sequence is the opposite: one place, thousands more quakes).

**8. The predictability meter.** The long-term goal is "exact time and place". The meter asks how far
we are: rank every (day, cell) by the forecast's M4+ rate and walk down until 90% of the real M4+ quakes
are caught. The share of space-time you walked through is the window. Watching everything is 100%; a
perfect forecast would be one cell on one day per quake.

**9. The referee.** A discovery must survive five tests: raw (Schuster's test), declustered (aftershocks
removed, Gardner-Knopoff windows), fake clocks (200 random periods, or for a computed tide, the same
tide slid by random lags), shuffled time of day, and a locked prediction (the effect hashed before a
held-out catalogue is read). Its first case was this project's own: on 2026-10-21, aftershocks of the
2016-17 sequence looked locked to the solid-Earth tide. Replayed with the computed tide: raw p = 10⁻⁴¹³.
Aftershocks removed (397 mainshocks left), p = 10⁻³·³: it survives. Locked and tested on 2009: p = 10⁻³·⁹, at
the same phase: it "predicts". Then the same tide slid by random lags, which can't line up with anything,
locks just as hard 71% of the time. The tide's phase is not spread evenly over time, so *any* set of moments
bunches on its favourite phases, and Schuster's test assumes it doesn't. Rejected. A true 12.42-hour signal
planted in a fake catalogue passes all five tests, and a fake catalogue with day-night detection (raw
p = 10⁻⁹³⁴) fails every one.

**10. Locked forecasts, in public.** Every night at 22:30 UTC a GitHub job fetches the INGV catalogue,
runs the frozen model of this release (18 Fields: three seeds of six) on it, and writes tomorrow's
forecast with ETAS's beside it, sealed with SHA-256, into `forecasts/`. Three days later, when INGV has
settled the catalogue, the day is graded. A locked file can never be edited: the check refuses it, and
GitHub keeps the time of the push. Missed days stay missed.

## The LLM analogy

- **Calibration / temperature scaling.** An LLM saying "90% sure" should be right 90% of the time; the
  negative binomial size is a one-parameter post-hoc fix, like temperature. Where it breaks: an LLM's
  classes are many and frequent, ours are rare, so the gap has to be read as a factor.
- **Deep ensembles.** Several networks from different seeds; disagreement as uncertainty.
- **OOD detection and refusal.** Nearest-neighbour distance in feature space, and abstaining past a
  threshold set on the training data alone. Where it breaks: this model's own feature space fails the
  test (below), so the detector leans on a hand-made fingerprint.
- **Red-teaming.** The fake precursors are adversarial inputs; the guard is the input filter.
- **Held-out evaluation and pre-registration.** The locked forecast is a pre-registered prediction,
  graded on data that did not exist when it was written.

## What the code does

1. `craton/trust/predictive.py`: a forecast as a distribution (members, negative binomial sizes).
2. `craton/trust/calibration.py`: reliability, the factor error, ECE, randomised PIT, CRPS, the fit.
3. `craton/trust/meter.py`: the predictability meter.
4. `craton/trust/physics.py`: the hard checks and the slip budget.
5. `craton/trust/attacks.py` and `craton/trust/guard.py`: the fakes, and the filter in front of the Field.
6. `craton/trust/ood.py`: translation, the trunk's vectors, the fingerprint, nearest neighbours.
7. `craton/bench/locks.py`: CB-05, CB-06, CB-07, CB-08, CB-11, through the same task registry as
   everything else.
8. `craton/field/forecaster.py`: six members, the magnitude ceiling, the guard, `predictive`.
9. `craton/trust/referee.py` and `craton/sim/`: the referee, and the fake planets its tests run on.
10. `craton/trust/lock.py`, `craton/live.py`, `.github/workflows/live.yml`: seal, ledger, grade, the
    nightly job.

In plain words:

- **The window shrank by a third.** All of it is in time: Craton knows better which days are dangerous.
  Where, on the day, it is level with ETAS.
- **Physics holds because of one line.** Magnitudes stop at 7.3 (the largest quake on record in the region,
  Avezzano 1915, was 7.1). ETAS spreads probability all the way to M8, and on calm days that alone promises
  more moment than the plate delivers.
- **The detector is mostly the fingerprint.** The trunk's own vectors score 0.12 to 0.38 on their own:
  worse than a coin, because the sequences inside central Italy look stranger to it than Sicily does, and it
  can't see depth. That is a finding about what the trunk learned, and the place P9's hidden state should
  improve.
- **Calibration: honest days, rough cells.** The day totals are the best of any method once the dispersion
  grows more slowly than the mean (fitted on validation days only). Cell by cell, Craton is a bit too high
  in the middle and too low at the top; ETAS-incomplete's 10% productivity boost fits the cells better.
- **The one exit test missed:** CB-07 at 1.002. Duplicates and the GNSS step change nothing; the
  magnitude shift slipped past the guard on one seed.
- **Every older number held.** The Field grew from P6's four members to six instead of being retrained, so
  CB-02 and CB-03 sit at or above P6.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. Why is the ECE a bad number for earthquake forecasts?</summary>

Because almost every cell-day has a tiny probability, and a gap between two tiny probabilities is
tiny even when one is three times the other. A factor (log₂ of observed over forecast) keeps "3x too
low" looking like what it is.
</details>

<details><summary>2. Why move every context to central Italy before the detector reads it?</summary>

The trunk reads absolute coordinates, so a context from Sicily is far from every training context
just by being somewhere else. That detector would score perfectly and tell you nothing about
whether the *seismicity* is familiar. Moved, only its character is left: depth, size, pace, spread.
</details>

<details><summary>3. The tide claim passed the declustered test and predicted 2009. Why is it still false?</summary>

Because the same tide slid by a random lag, which can't be aligned with any quake, locks just as
hard. The tide's phase is not spread evenly over time, so any set of moments clusters on its
favourite phases; Schuster's test assumes an evenly spread phase. Shifted tides are the null that
keeps that unevenness and breaks the alignment.
</details>

<details><summary>4. Why aren't the swarm and the b-value drop counted in CB-07?</summary>

Because they are real-looking seismicity. A real swarm does raise the chance of a bigger quake, and
ETAS says so; a forecaster that ignored it would be worse. What it must not do is react to things
that say nothing about the Earth: a GNSS step it doesn't read, a quake listed twice, a miscalibrated
magnitude.
</details>

<details><summary>5. Why seal forecasts with a hash and push them before the day?</summary>

So nobody, including us, can improve a forecast after seeing what happened. The hash proves the file
wasn't touched; GitHub's record of the push proves when it was written.
</details>

## Glossary

- **Reliability / calibration**: whether forecast probabilities match how often things happen.
- **ECE**: expected calibration error, the size-weighted gap between forecast and observed.
- **PIT**: where the real outcome falls inside the forecast's distribution; uniform if honest.
- **Negative binomial**: a count distribution with more spread than Poisson (variance mean + mean²/k).
- **Epistemic vs aleatoric**: "the model doesn't know" vs "the dice haven't been rolled yet".
- **OOD (out of distribution)**: inputs unlike the training data.
- **Kostrov moment rate**: the seismic moment a deforming belt can release per year.
- **Schuster test**: is a set of phases bunched? p = exp(−R²/N).
- **Declustering**: removing aftershocks so the rest are closer to independent.
- **Shifted tides**: the tide slid in time, a null that keeps its rhythm and breaks its alignment.
- **Locked forecast**: sealed and published before the period it forecasts.
