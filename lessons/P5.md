# P5 — Fusion: quakes + GPS + the Moon

## TLDR

Craton-mini now reads three instruments at once: the earthquake catalogue, daily GPS positions and the
solid-Earth tide. One trunk, one encoder per instrument, every token in the same space.

Hide a day of a GPS station and it puts it back to within a few millimetres: **9.5 bits** per hidden
day, where guessing from the station's history costs 22.8. That takes CB-01 from 21.66 to **15.12**.
It rebuilds a GPS station it has never seen (CB-04) a little better than its neighbours' weighted
average, and it adapts to a region it has never seen from that region's first year of quakes (CB-17),
though plain counting still adapts better.

The ablation measures what each instrument is worth. GPS: thirteen bits on GPS, nothing on quakes. The
tide: zero, within noise, everywhere. That's what the science says to expect, and now it's measured.

## The idea, step by step

**1. One trunk, several dictionaries.** An LLM has one vocabulary. Craton has one encoder per
instrument: a quake is (latitude, longitude, depth, magnitude), a GPS day is how far a station moved,
a tide sample is how far the ground rose. Each encoder maps its own numbers to the same 256-wide
vector, and adds a learned "I am a GPS day" vector on top. After that, the trunk can't tell where a
token came from except by what it says. That's how image-text models work: one encoder for pictures,
one for words, one shared space.

**2. A GPS position is a useless number until you subtract.** A station's east coordinate is millions
of millimetres from an arbitrary origin, and it creeps a few centimetres a year as Italy moves. Feed
that raw number to a network and it sees noise. So every station gets a **line**: where it was in 2015
and how fast it moves, fitted on the training data. A day enters as its distance from the line, in
centimetres. Then each station's days in the window are centred on their own average (the *level*),
and the level goes in as a channel of its own. Time-series forecasters call this reversible instance
normalisation (Kim et al., ICLR 2022). It also keeps a station that jumped (an earthquake, a new
antenna) readable.

**3. The answer is a distribution, in millimetres.** For a hidden GPS day the head gives, per
direction, a mixture of four bell-shaped (logistic) curves. CB-01 scores it on 1 mm bins, and the
training loss is that exact score, the same trick as P4.

**4. A station nobody has seen borrows a line.** For CB-04 a station is hidden completely: no
position, no velocity. It borrows its velocity from its six nearest neighbours, weighted by
1/distance², the way the IDW baseline does, and the head has a *ramp* (millimetres per year) to
correct it.

**5. A window has a budget, and a policy.** 1,024 tokens, filled in order: 25 hours of tide around the
moment, the GPS days of the nearest stations, then quakes nearest in time. A forecast window also reads
the tide of the day ahead (it's computed from the Sun and the Moon, so it's known in advance) and keeps
the year's 32 biggest quakes even when they're old, because big quakes trigger for longer.

**6. Hide one kind, guess it from the others.** Cross-modal masking: hide every GPS day in a window
and predict the ground from the quakes and the tide; hide every quake and predict them from the
ground. That's the plan's step 4, and it's built and tested. Then it was measured, and switched off for
now (step 8).

**7. Your own question: isn't this what attention is for?** Attention decides which tokens in a
window to listen to, and it does ignore the useless ones. What it can't do is use a token that isn't
in the window, or get good at a game it barely plays. The first fused model scored 16.05 on CB-01,
six bits better than P4, but only because GPS carried it. Its hidden-quake half had got worse, 22.47
against P4's 20.49. The ratchet would have accepted it. It didn't ship.

**8. The data mix decides what a model is good at.** The tests, one change at a time, quake half only:

| Training | hidden quake, bits |
|---|---|
| quakes only (P4 rebuilt) | 20.54 |
| fused, 55% quake windows, 45% GPS windows | 21.68 |
| fused, 85% / 15% | 21.51 |
| **fused, 100% quake windows** | **20.17** |

Reading GPS and tides makes the model *better* at quakes. Playing GPS-centred games costs the quakes
more than a bit, at every dose: a 4.7-million-parameter model doesn't have room for both. So the general
models play the quake game (which still hides 15% of each window's GPS days), and the CB-17 model adds
the region game it's tested on. The other games wait for a bigger model. LLM labs face the same
choice: mixture weights.

**9. A bug that looked like physics.** Each token carries a clock, "how long since the start of the
window". The rebuild measured it from the window's middle, so an hour before and an hour after looked
the same. Fixed: 20.78 → 20.54 on the quakes-only model.

**10. When the model has never seen anything this big, let physics drive.** The 2009 forecast model
learns from data up to 2008, which holds one big sequence. Its three seeds expected 287, 1,412 and 872
quakes for 2009 (315 happened): each invented its own extrapolation. The rate head has two paths, a
straight line from the measured recent rates (Omori-shaped) and a free correction from the trunk. The
correction now moves the forecast at most 2× either way. The three seeds now expect 225, 273 and 210,
and CB-03 beats P4.

## The LLM analogy

| LLM | Craton P5 | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Image + text encoders into one space | one encoder per instrument | instruments have units and physics; pixels don't |
| Token budget, context selection (RAG) | the context policy | ours is hand-written; RAG learns what to fetch |
| Data mixture weights | the window mix | at 4.7M parameters, every game competes for room |
| In-context learning | CB-17 | here, counting the context still beats the model |
| Ablation studies | `craton bench ablate` | three seeds per variant: noise is wide |

## What the code does

1. `craton/model/encoders.py`: the registry (an instrument's id is its place in it, forever), the quake
   encoder, `ScalarEncoder` for GPS and tides.
2. `craton/model/craton.py`: `Tokens` (instrument id, up to 12 numbers, hidden flags), every encoder
   run over every token, each token keeping its own instrument's answer.
3. `craton/train/context.py`: station lines, the policy, `write()` that turns windows into tensors and
   lists every hidden token's answer.
4. `craton/train/data.py`: the window kinds (quake, GPS, region) and next-day questions.
5. `craton/model/heads.py`: the GPS head, and the capped rate correction.
6. `craton/train/method.py`: how each benchmark is answered.
7. `craton/bench/ablation.py`: the ablation and its table.

## The numbers

Mean ± spread over three seeds (run `20261021-122053-craton-mini`). Lower is better for CB-01, CB-04,
CB-17; higher for CB-02, CB-03.

| Task | P5 | P4 | Floor | In plain words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CB-01 | **15.12 ± 0.37** | 21.66 | climatology 22.66 | six bits less surprised per hidden measurement |
| · a hidden quake | 20.48 | 20.49 | 22.48 | a tie: fusion didn't cost the quakes anything |
| · a hidden GPS day | 9.75 | (climatology) | 22.83 | about ±3 mm per direction, from the station's other days |
| CB-02 | −10.98 ± 0.77 | −10.88 ± 0.67 | ETAS −7.12 | the same within noise; ETAS still wins by 4 bits a quake |
| CB-03 | −14.05 ± 0.31 | −15.13 ± 0.79 | ETAS −9.40 | better and steadier; ETAS still wins |
| CB-04 | **17.50 ± 0.04** mm | none | IDW 17.77 | a quarter of a millimetre better than the neighbours' average |
| CB-15 | **16.27%** MFU | 15.59% | 6 GiB gate | faster per FLOP, 1.4 GB peak |
| CB-17 | 24.56 ± 0.56 | none | counting 19.03, climatology 27.72 | learns 7.9 bits from a year of context; counting learns more |

What didn't move: the forecasts. GPS and the tide don't help CB-02 beyond noise (the ablation), and
ETAS is still four bits a quake ahead. Most of CB-04's error is one station, VCRA, whose series jumps
by up to 31 cm in a day: no neighbour can see that coming, so no method will.

```bash

```

## Check yourself

<details><summary>Why can't the model read a GPS position as it comes?</summary>
It's millions of millimetres from an arbitrary origin and drifts centimetres a year: the signal is in
the changes. Distance from the station's own line, centred on its window, is the change.
</details>

<details><summary>The first fused model beat P4 on CB-01 by six bits. Why didn't it ship?</summary>
CB-01 is the mean of two halves. GPS gained 13 bits, quakes lost 2; the mean hid the loss.
</details>

<details><summary>Why is the tide's share zero, and is that a failure?</summary>
Tidal triggering is real but tiny (a small phase effect across hundreds of thousands of quakes,
Métivier et al. 2009). On one region's daily scores it doesn't show. A measured zero is a result; P8's
planted test asks the sharper question.
</details>

<details><summary>What is CB-17 actually testing?</summary>
Whether the model learns a place from context alone, with no retraining, the way an LLM picks up a
pattern from a prompt.
</details>

## Glossary

- **Encoder**: the small network that turns one instrument's numbers into a token vector.
- **Modality embedding**: the learned "which instrument am I" vector added to every token.
- **Station line**: a GPS station's position in 2015 plus its velocity, fitted on training data.
- **Instance normalisation**: centring a series on its own window before the model reads it.
- **Cross-modal masking**: hiding one instrument and predicting it from the others.
- **Ablation**: retraining without one part to measure what it was worth.
- **In-context learning**: adapting from what's in the input, not from retraining.
